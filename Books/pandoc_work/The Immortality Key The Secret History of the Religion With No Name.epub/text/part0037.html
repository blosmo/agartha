Whether Italy was introduced to wine drinking by the Ancient Greeks, or the taste developed among the Etruscans and other indigenous people prior to colonization, Komar summarizes the current state of the debate: “it is possible to form a hypothesis that the inhabitants of Italy in the Bronze Age were interested in vines and could make fermented beverage from its fruits. It was, however, rather primitive and on a small scale till the arrival of the Greeks, who taught them how to take proper care of vitis (pruning introduced by Numa in the times of Greek colonization of the Apennine peninsula). They also spread the idea of wine drinking during the symposion (Greek wine ware dominating in archaeological evidence).”
